Permissions: Who May Do What (Architecture)

This document describes how access is granted and read in the portal: the permission catalogue, the team permission sets and their presets, the authentication backend that derives a person's permissions from their teams, and how to add a permission. It is the reference for any work that touches volunteer/auth_backends.py, volunteer/permission_presets.py, an app's permissions.py, or a Meta.permissions block. The reasoning behind the design is in the speaker portal architecture document, section 7.1.

The rule

Every "may this person do this" check goes through Django permissions: user.has_perm("app.codename") in Python, wrapped in a named predicate in the owning app's permissions.py, and {% if perms.app.codename %} in templates. Nothing reads is_staff. The flag survives for one purpose, signing in to the Django admin, and is_superuser keeps Django's built-in bypass as break-glass.

Two things are deliberately not permissions, because they are relations in the data: leading a particular team (team.team_leads), and looking after a particular presenter (Presenter.liaison), carrying an item (assignee or an owning team), or being the presenter yourself. Those stay object-level checks beside the permission checks.

The catalogue

Custom permissions are declared in Meta.permissions on the model that owns the feature; the built-in add, change, delete and view ones are reused where they fit. volunteer.permission_presets.CATALOGUE carries the same labels in plain words, grouped by app, and is what the pickers and the pages show.

Teams grant

A Team carries two sets of auth.Permission rows: permissions, for every approved member, and lead_permissions, which the team leads hold on top. Both are edited on the team form in the portal as grouped checkbox lists in plain words, with a "Start from preset" select that fills them. Only whoever holds portal.view_organizer_dashboard, which only the Organizers preset carries (or a superuser), may change the sets (volunteer.organizers.may_change_grants); it is read as a permission, never from a team's name, which any team editor can set. For anyone else holding change_team the pickers are disabled, since they could otherwise grant themselves anything through their own team. On the review form, a reviewer adds someone to a team or removes them only when they hold every permission that team grants its members (holds_all); on the team form, who leads a team is changed only by someone who holds all of its lead permissions; and no reviewer reviews their own application. The team page lists what the team may do, and a person's volunteer profile page lists what they hold and where each grant comes from. Start next year clones both sets with the teams.

The presets, in volunteer.permission_presets.PRESETS:

Team Members Leads add
Organizers every catalogue permission except the two below delete_conference, start_next_year
Volunteer coordination view and review volunteers, manage teams, volunteer notifications
Sponsorship sponsor add/change/delete, the contract request, unconfirmed statuses, tiers, sponsorship notifications
Program manage and view the program, assign items, liaise, templates, guides, review proposals, publish the program, view, upload and edit files, publish session videos change_speakersettings, delete files
Proposal reviewers view the program, review proposals
Speaker liaisons liaise presenters, assign items
Recordings view the program, view, upload and edit files, publish session videos delete files, assign items

A team with no set at all is still a team: work reaches it through task assignment, which is relational.

A backend derives

volunteer.auth_backends.TeamPermissionsBackend, third in AUTHENTICATION_BACKENDS, authenticates nobody. It answers get_all_permissions from the user's approved memberships and leaderships in the active edition's teams, plus one baseline tied to no team: an approved volunteer of the active edition may read sponsors (volunteer.constants.BASELINE_APPROVED_VOLUNTEER). The result is cached on the user instance for the request. It also answers with_perm, so recipient queries find team members.

Because the backend reads the active edition, the edition switch revokes by itself: last year's Sponsorship team grants nothing today, and with no edition active only a superuser reaches Organize. That is the reason the grants are not synced into global auth.Group rows.

Groups still exist for capabilities that outlive an edition. Today that is Infra maintainers. The Django admin's group page offers members as a side-by-side box limited to this year's approved, verified volunteers plus the current members, and labels a member who no longer qualifies, so a group can be reviewed and pruned from its own page.

Django's User.objects.with_perm refuses to answer while more than one backend can, so recipient queries go through common.permissions.users_with_perm, which asks Django's backend and the team backend together.

Adding a permission

  1. Declare it in Meta.permissions on the model that owns the feature, and write the migration.
  2. Add its label and plain wording to CATALOGUE, and to the presets whose teams should carry it. Add the same label to the literal list in the next data migration only if a seeded team must gain it on deploy.
  3. Add a named predicate to the app's permissions.py and read it from the view (Django's PermissionRequiredMixin with the label, or the predicate in test_func) and from the template (perms.app.codename).
  4. Test the grant through a preset team (join_team in the root conftest), the denial for someone without it, and that a staff flag alone opens nothing.

What is no longer read

The volunteer Role model and VolunteerProfile.roles stay in the schema, because they are upstream's, but nothing reads or shows them. Roles granted no access; the Admin and Staff ones routed the internal emails and picked the organizer variant of the onboarding email, both of which read permissions now. The migration put every Admin and Staff holder on their edition's Organizers team. Rosters, profiles and emails show teams and a Lead badge instead, and the Django admin no longer lists roles.
